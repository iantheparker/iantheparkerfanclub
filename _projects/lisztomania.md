<div class="intro-txt">
<p>
I've always loved remix culture and in early 2009 it was having a moment. The Internet was
appropriating and mashing up every piece of pop culture they could find in the hopes that
something interesting would come of it. I made something that kinda blew up and became a meme.
<br>
<span class="subtitle">Concept</span>
<span class="subtitle">Director</span>
<span class="subtitle">Editor</span>
</p>
</div>

<div class="text-block" markdown="1">

I saw this on YouTube, which cut 1980's Brat Pack movie scenes to Phoenix's catchy new song, Lisztomania.

</div>

{% include youtube.html id="XtE-xnPKj28" %}

<div class="text-block" markdown="1">

I was mesmerized. It was totally trivial, yet a surprisingly touching tribute to the 80s.
I couldn't get it out of my head and I must have watched it 50 times within the first two
days. By the third day I was convinced I needed to make a tribute video to this tribute video
to keep this meta-joke going. On the fourth day, I grabbed some friends, gave them a case of
wine, put them on a Brooklyn rooftop during an epic sunset, and pressed record. I edited it
all down, put it online the next day, and titled it the Brooklyn Bratpack Mashup.

</div>

{% include youtube.html id="U1ywFh2AZLg" %}

<div class="text-block" markdown="1">

video. It blew our minds. It felt amazing. Shortly after that, we had one from

<div class="text-block" markdown="1">

Then, the cherry on top: Professor Larry Lessig, the father of Creative Commons and champion
of remix culture, highlighted us in a TED talk called "Re-examining the remix". Thanks Lessig.
I feel internet famous now.

**Update 1/3/2019:**

This meme just became hot again after alt-right trolls discovered that NY Congressional
Representative [Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Ocasio-Cortez)
was in the Boston University remix and tried to use footage of her happy dancing to shame her.
I totally endorse your dance moves AOC.
